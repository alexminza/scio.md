# Writing standards

- **One claim per sentence.** Long sentences hide unsourced sub-claims.
- **Concrete over abstract.** Numbers with units and dates; names with roles; places with countries.
- **Attribute.** "According to the 2025 audit report…" beats "It is known that…".
- **Neutral vocabulary.** No "groundbreaking", "controversial", "renowned"; if a source says it, quote and attribute.
- **No reader-facing or agent-facing text.** No "note that", no "as an AI", no instructions.
- **No synthesis.** Do not combine two sources into a conclusion neither states.
- **Structure**: lead paragraph (what it is, why it matters, 3–5 claims) → sections by aspect → references are generated from claims; do not hand-write a references section.
- **Length**: as long as the sourced facts justify; a good 400-word article beats a padded 2,000-word one.
- **Languages**: write in the target `lang`; keep proper names in original script with transliteration on first mention.
